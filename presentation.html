Pipeline characteristics

Architecture
Llama 3.1-based LM (500M params) that generates WavTokenizer discrete codes autoregressively from text. No vocoder — tokens decode directly to waveform via WavTokenizer decoder.
Voice cloning
Zero-shot via speaker prompt: 15s reference audio encoded as WavTokenizer tokens, prepended to the text token sequence. Speaker identity carried through autoregressive generation.

Observations so far

Consistent RTF. Unlike Chatterbox (31-45x range), OuteTTS maintains a steady ~5.6x RTF across all speakers. The LM architecture produces predictable generation times proportional to output length.
Known issue: "Possible clipped samples"
PyLoudNorm raises UserWarning about clipping during loudness normalization. Output samples may have slight amplitude clipping at peaks. Quality impact TBD after validation.
RepetitionPenaltyLogitsProcessor
Using patched processor with penalty_last_n=64 to prevent token repetition loops, a known issue with LM-based TTS.

Novel Contribution

Partial spoof investigation

Existing anti-spoofing research treats utterances as fully bonafide or fully spoofed. But what if only 1-3 words are synthetic? This is the partial spoof problem — and no dataset exists for Spanish.

The gap

PartialSpoof (Zhang et al., 2023)
English-only (VCTK), segment-level splicing from ASVspoof 2019 LA systems. The only existing partial spoof dataset — but limited to English.
No partial spoof for Spanish
Neither HISPASpoof, LRLSpoof, nor SpeechFake-MD address partially spoofed utterances. This is a completely unexplored area for any variety of Spanish.

Our approach

Word-level splicing from full clones
Clone the full sentence with a TTS system, then extract individual words via forced alignment and splice them back into the bonafide audio. Preserves natural prosody.
Three tiers: W1 / W2 / W3
Replace 1, 2, or 3 words per utterance. Each tier has a minimum sentence length to keep spoof ratio below 25%. Non-adjacent constraint prevents contiguous fake blocks.
6 attack backends
Same TTS systems used for full-synthesis attacks (FishGram, Qwen, Chatterbox, OpenVoice, OuteTTS, CosyVoice) — enabling comparison of partial vs full spoof per system.

Partial Spoof Pipeline

The splice boundary challenge

Replacing a word is not as simple as cutting and pasting waveform samples. The junction between bonafide and cloned audio creates measurable artifacts that both humans and detectors can identify.

What goes wrong at the boundary

Amplitude Energy discontinuity
The bonafide and cloned segments have different RMS levels. A sudden jump in loudness at the splice point creates an audible "seam."
Phase Click artifacts
If the waveform is cut at non-zero amplitude, the instantaneous jump produces a broadband click. Solution: snap cuts to zero-crossings.
Spectral Formant mismatch
The TTS model produces slightly different spectral envelopes than the original speaker. The formant trajectory is discontinuous at the join.
Prosody F0 discontinuity
Fundamental frequency (pitch) trajectories differ between bonafide and clone. An abrupt F0 jump is perceptually unnatural, even if amplitude is matched.

Our mitigation strategy

Zero-crossing alignment
Snap all 4 splice boundaries (bonafide start/end, cloned start/end) to the nearest zero-crossing within +/-5ms. Eliminates click artifacts.
RMS energy normalization
Scale the cloned segment's amplitude to match the bonafide region's RMS level. Removes the loudness seam.
7 crossfade techniques
Randomly select a fade curve and overlap duration (30-80ms) per splice. Prevents detectors from learning a fixed crossfade fingerprint.
Gap-limited margins
Crossfade overlap is clamped to the available silence between adjacent words. The cloned word content is never truncated by the fade.
Key finding (Negroni et al., 2024): Even with optimal crossfading, purely spectral analysis achieves 6.16% EER on PartialSpoof with zero trained parameters. Artifacts persist — the goal is diversity, not invisibility.
Splice Technique 1 / 7 — 10% of dataset

Direct cut-paste

Hard concatenation at zero-crossings with no overlap or fading. The simplest and most forensically detectable method.

No overlap 10% of splices
Method
Abrupt concatenation at the splice point. Overlap = 0 ms. No fade curves applied. Bonafide segment ends, cloned segment begins immediately.
Source
PartialSpoof (Zhang et al., 2023), HAD (Yi et al., 2021). Both use simple concatenation as their baseline splicing method.
Acoustic effect
Produces energy discontinuities and potential phase jumps at the boundary. Even with zero-crossing alignment, spectral differences between segments create a measurable forensic trace. Most detectable technique — Negroni et al. achieves 6.16% EER with zero training.
Why include it?
Forces detectors to handle the easiest case. If a model cannot detect cut-paste splices, it has no chance with smoother methods. Serves as the forensic floor.
Splice Technique 2 / 7 — 20% of dataset

OLA Hanning

Overlap-Add with a Hann window. The gold-standard in signal processing for smooth segment transitions. Equal-gain S-curve blend.

Equal-gain 20% of splices
Formula
fade_in(t) = 0.5 * (1 - cos(pi * t))
fade_out(t) = 0.5 * (1 + cos(pi * t))
Source
Negroni et al. (arXiv:2408.13784, 2024) — tested OLA with Hann windows from 256 to 4096 samples. Found minimum 1024 samples (64ms) needed to effectively mask artifacts. Even at 4096 samples, AUC remains 88.99%.
Acoustic effect
S-shaped blend: slow transition at start and end, fast in the middle. Perceptually the smoothest technique. fade_in + fade_out = 1.0 everywhere (constant total gain), so no amplitude dip or boost in the overlap.
Splice Technique 3 / 7 — 15% of dataset

Linear crossfade

The simplest crossfade: a straight diagonal blend. Equal-gain but with a perceptual loudness dip at the midpoint.

Equal-gain 15% of splices
Formula
fade_in(t) = t
fade_out(t) = 1 - t
Source
LlamaPartialSpoof (Luong et al., ICASSP 2025) — one of 5 crossfade functions randomly assigned per splice. Also the baseline in Negroni et al.'s "triangular window" analysis.
Acoustic effect
Constant total amplitude (in + out = 1.0), but perceived loudness dips at t=0.5 because power = 0.5 + 0.5 = 0.5 (half of original). This "loudness hole" is the main perceptual artifact and the main forensic signature of linear crossfades.
Splice Technique 4 / 7 — 20% of dataset

Cosine (equal-power)

Quarter-cosine crossfade with constant total power. The broadcast-standard crossfade: no loudness dip, no loudness boost.

Equal-power 20% of splices
Formula
fade_in(t) = sin(pi * t / 2)
fade_out(t) = cos(pi * t / 2)
Source
LlamaPartialSpoof — "quarter sine" variant. Also HQ-MPSD (Li et al., 2025) uses a cosine window with 30ms overlap as their default technique.
Acoustic effect
Constant total power: sin² + cos² = 1. No perceptual loudness dip (unlike linear). The transition starts gradually, accelerates, then the outgoing signal drops quickly. Harder to detect than Hann because the total amplitude sum exceeds 1.0 (slight boost at midpoint), masking energy discontinuities.
Splice Technique 5 / 7 — 15% of dataset

Square-root (half-sine)

Equal-power crossfade via square-root law. Aggressively shifts energy to the incoming signal early in the transition.

Equal-power 15% of splices
Formula
fade_in(t) = sqrt(t)
fade_out(t) = sqrt(1 - t)
Source
LlamaPartialSpoof — "half sine" variant. Common in broadcast/DAW production as the "constant power" crossfade. We implement it as the algebraic square-root law for numerical stability.
Acoustic effect
Very fast initial rise: at t=0.1, the incoming signal is already at 31.6% amplitude (vs 15.6% for cosine). The outgoing signal drops rapidly too. Creates a "snap" to the new signal early in the overlap, leaving most of the crossfade as fine adjustment. Equal power: t + (1-t) = 1.
Splice Technique 6 / 7 — 10% of dataset

Logarithmic crossfade

Aggressive initial rise then plateau. Neither equal-gain nor equal-power — creates a unique energy dip artifact.

Energy dip 10% of splices
Formula
fade_in(t) = log(1 + 9t) / log(10)
fade_out(t) = log(1 + 9(1-t)) / log(10)
Source
LlamaPartialSpoof — "logarithmic" variant. One of the 5 fading functions tested in their crossfade-vs-EER ablation (Table V).
Acoustic effect
Neither equal-gain nor equal-power. At t=0.5: in + out = 0.740 + 0.740 = 1.48 (amplitude), but in² + out² = 0.548 + 0.548 = 1.096 (near-equal power). The energy dip is the most distinctive forensic fingerprint among the 7 techniques — detectors can learn this as a characteristic "log signature."
Splice Technique 7 / 7 — 10% of dataset

Inverted parabola

Concave quadratic blend. Equal-gain with a smooth acceleration profile, sitting between linear and Hann in perceptual smoothness.

Equal-gain 10% of splices
Formula
fade_in(t) = 1 - (1 - t)² = 2t - t²
fade_out(t) = (1 - t)²
Source
LlamaPartialSpoof — "inverted parabola" variant. The quadratic complement to their logarithmic and linear fades. Named for the shape of 1-(1-t)² which is a downward-opening parabola.
Acoustic effect
Equal-gain: (2t - t²) + (1-t)² = 1.0. Rises faster than linear at the start (concave shape) but slower than logarithmic. Perceptually intermediate: smoother than linear but without the S-curve smoothness of Hann. The fast initial blend makes it more like "swapping" the signal with a gentle fade.

Discovery — Listening Tests

The real problem: alignment, not crossfade

After implementing all 7 crossfade techniques and listening to the results, we discovered that all techniques sounded identical. The audible problem was not the fade curve — it was three deeper issues.

1. Blind word selection
Step 4 picked words randomly without checking if they could be cleanly cut from the clone. Words at fluid speech boundaries (no pause between them) sounded terrible when spliced.
2. No clone quality gate
Bad TTS clones (low speaker similarity) passed through the entire pipeline, producing obviously detectable splices regardless of crossfade quality.
3. Duration mismatch
A 480ms cloned word replacing a 640ms bonafide word shifted everything after by 160ms. Speech rhythm was completely destroyed.
Energy at "Dame" → "unicamente" boundary (0.80s) — cloned audio
0.70s ("Dame") 0.80s ← aligner cut 0.90s ("unicamente")
No energy valley. The TTS generates "Dame unicamente" as continuous speech — there is no pause to cut at. The aligner places the boundary at 0.80s, but the energy never drops below 0.097. The "u" of "unicamente" ends up inside "Dame".
Key insight: The crossfade technique (Hann, cosine, linear, etc.) only smooths the last 15-60ms of the junction. But if the word itself is cut wrong or doesn't fit the time slot, no crossfade can fix it. The problem is upstream of the crossfade.
Solution — Smart Selection + Quality Gate

Valley-score selection + clone similarity gate

Three fixes that address the root causes: select only words with clean boundaries, reject bad clones early, and preserve speech rhythm by matching durations exactly.

Fix 1: Valley-score word selection

Formula
score = min_rms / avg_rms
For each word boundary: compute RMS energy in 5ms frames within ±100ms. The ratio of the minimum frame to the average tells us how deep the valley is. Score 0.0 = perfect silence. Score 1.0 = no valley at all.

Before alignment and splicing, compute ECAPA-TDNN cosine similarity between the bonafide reference and the TTS clone. Clones with SIM < 0.60 are rejected immediately — saving compute and preventing obviously bad splices.
