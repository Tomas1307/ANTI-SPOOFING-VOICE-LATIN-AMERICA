Problem & Motivation

Two gaps, one corpus

Voice anti-spoofing decides whether a speech signal is genuine (bonafide) or machine-generated (spoofed). The reference resource for Latin American Spanish — HABLA (Interspeech 2023) — is three years stale, and the field still frames spoofing as all-or-nothing. HABLA 2.0 closes both gaps at once.

Gap A — an outdated corpus
HABLA is 3+ years old
GAN-only attacks (StarGAN-VC, CycleGAN-VC) — deprecated 2018–2020 technology.
No augmentation — models trained on clean studio audio collapse under reverb, codec compression, and noise.
No neural codec / flow-matching TTS — the dominant 2025–2026 synthesis paradigm is entirely absent.
English detectors hit EER >45% on Spanish — cross-lingual transfer fails.
Gap B — the partial-spoof blind spot new
Every corpus is all-or-nothing
All anti-spoofing datasets — HABLA included — contain fully-synthetic OR fully-bonafide audio only.
The real threat is partial: splice a few cloned words into genuine speech ("transfer 100"→"transfer 9000"). 90%+ of the signal is real, so global detectors miss it.
No Latin American Spanish partial-spoof corpus exists.
Detectors learn a shortcut — the noisy splice boundary, not real synthesis artifacts (Negroni 2024: 6.16% EER, zero training). It does not generalize.
HABLA's regional accents remain valuable. Its attack set, its lack of augmentation, and its all-or-nothing framing do not. HABLA 2.0 modernizes the attacks, hardens robustness, and introduces the first Spanish partial-spoof corpus with adversarial boundary augmentation.
