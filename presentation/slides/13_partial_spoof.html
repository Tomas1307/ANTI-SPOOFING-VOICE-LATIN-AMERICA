Novel Contribution — Partial Spoof

Word-level partial spoof for Spanish

First partial spoof dataset for Latin American Spanish. Unlike full-synthesis attacks, partial spoof replaces 1-3 individual words in bonafide audio with voice-cloned versions — making detection significantly harder. Inspired by Zhang et al. (IEEE/ACM TASLP 2023) but adapted for Spanish with modern TTS systems.

Highlighted words extracted from TTS clone and spliced into bonafide audio with 30-80ms crossfade (7 techniques)
Why full-sentence cloning first? Words generated in isolation have flat, citation-form prosody. By cloning the full sentence, then extracting target words via forced alignment, the spliced words carry natural in-context prosody — making the partial spoof harder to detect and scientifically more valuable.

7-step pipeline

01
Transcribe bonafide
Parakeet TDT 0.6b-v3 with word-level timestamps
02
Clone full sentence
6 attack strategies (Factory pattern): FishGram, Qwen, Chatterbox, OpenVoice, OuteTTS, CosyVoice
03
Forced alignment
Word timestamps for both bonafide and cloned versions
04
Select words
Random non-adjacent selection per tier (seeded RNG)
05
Splice audio
7 splice techniques, 30-80ms overlap, per-splice random selection
06
Validate quality
Parakeet WER/CER + NISQA MOS + ECAPA-TDNN speaker similarity
07
Format to ASVspoof2019 LA
FLAC output with partial_spoof label in protocol
vs PartialSpoof (Zhang et al., 2023): English-only (VCTK), segment-level splicing from ASVspoof 2019 LA systems. We use word-level extraction from modern TTS (2024-2026), across 7 Latin American accents with 1,567 speakers. First partial spoof dataset for any variety of Spanish.
