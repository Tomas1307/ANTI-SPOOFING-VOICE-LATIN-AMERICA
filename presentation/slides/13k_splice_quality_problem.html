Discovery — Listening Tests

The real problem: alignment, not crossfade

After implementing all 7 crossfade techniques and listening to the results, we discovered that all techniques sounded identical. The audible problem was not the fade curve — it was three deeper issues.

1. Blind word selection
Step 4 picked words randomly without checking if they could be cleanly cut from the clone. Words at fluid speech boundaries (no pause between them) sounded terrible when spliced.
2. No clone quality gate
Bad TTS clones (low speaker similarity) passed through the entire pipeline, producing obviously detectable splices regardless of crossfade quality.
3. Duration mismatch
A 480ms cloned word replacing a 640ms bonafide word shifted everything after by 160ms. Speech rhythm was completely destroyed.
Energy at "Dame" → "unicamente" boundary (0.80s) — cloned audio
0.70s ("Dame") 0.80s ← aligner cut 0.90s ("unicamente")
No energy valley. The TTS generates "Dame unicamente" as continuous speech — there is no pause to cut at. The aligner places the boundary at 0.80s, but the energy never drops below 0.097. The "u" of "unicamente" ends up inside "Dame".
Key insight: The crossfade technique (Hann, cosine, linear, etc.) only smooths the last 15-60ms of the junction. But if the word itself is cut wrong or doesn't fit the time slot, no crossfade can fix it. The problem is upstream of the crossfade.
