Partial Spoof — Boundary Jitter (Step 5b)

Homogenize boundary anomalies so the splice no longer stands out

After Step 5 splices the cloned word, the only unusual boundaries are the two around the spoof. A detector exploits "find the noisy boundary" as a shortcut. Step 5b applies the same kind of structural manipulations to every internal boundary so the spoof no longer stands out.

Negroni et al. (2024): 6.16 % EER on PartialSpoof with zero training, purely by analyzing dynamic-range discontinuity at the splice join. Muller (2024): detectors generalize via dataset-specific shortcuts (silence length, spectral cleanness), not synthesis features.

Per-boundary independent coin flip

for each internal boundary i (right-to-left):
  if rng.uniform() < JITTER_PROBABILITY = 0.5:
    op = uniform_pick(truncate, overlap, bleed)
    apply(audio, op, uniform_magnitude_within_range)
  else: leave boundary natural
Spoof boundaries receive the same coin flip on top of the splice. Seed = JITTER_SEED + sha256(splice_key)[:4] for reproducibility.

Why p = 0.5: count-distribution mixing

Before jitter (deterministic)
Boundary typeManipulations
bonafide-bonafide0 (always)
spoof1 (always)
Detector classifies trivially on count.
After jitter (p = 0.5)
Boundary typeDistribution over count
bonafide-bonafide0 (50 %) or 1 (50 %)
spoof1 (50 %) or 2 (50 %)
Both share equal mass at count = 1 → ambiguous.
Word interiors stay intact. All three operations act only at boundaries -- never inside a word. With p = 0.5 per boundary, the probability that both flanking boundaries of a word flip natural is 0.25. By construction, ~25 % of non-spoof words remain bit-identical to the bonafide. Intelligibility is preserved; only the boundary fingerprint is homogenized.
