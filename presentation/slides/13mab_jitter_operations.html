Boundary Jitter — Three Structural Operations

Truncate, overlap, bleed: chosen uniformly when the coin flips heads

Each operation imitates a specific splice artifact, so a manipulated bonafide-bonafide boundary is acoustically indistinguishable from a real splice boundary at the spectral level. Magnitudes are drawn uniformly at random within ranges anchored to Spanish phonetics and to the LlamaPartialSpoof crossfade convention.

Operation Magnitude Effect on audio Mimics splice artifact
truncate 10 - 40 ms Cut left tail or right head → abrupt onset/offset Hard cut/paste splice (no crossfade)
overlap 30 - 80 ms Sum left tail with right head, Hanning fade OLA-Hanning crossfade splice
bleed 20 - 60 ms Insert fragment of one word into the other → pre/post-echo Tail bleed of crossfade splice

Per-boundary probability table

P(natural) = 0.500
P(truncate left_tail) = 0.083
P(truncate right_head) = 0.083
P(overlap hanning) = 0.167
P(bleed L→R) = 0.083
P(bleed R→L) = 0.083
total = 1.000
Uniform pick among ops because we have no prior knowledge of which artifact type the detector is most sensitive to — that would require running the detector first (circular).

Magnitude grounding

Overlap (30-80 ms) matches the LlamaPartialSpoof crossfade range (Luong et al. 2024) exactly. Same temporal scale as the splice itself.
Truncate (10-40 ms) stays below Spanish syllable nucleus duration (~50-90 ms) so intelligibility is preserved. 10 ms minimum is at the threshold of audibility.
Bleed (20-60 ms) covers Spanish VOT for voiceless stops (4-29 ms) plus consonant-vowel transition — inserted fragment sounds like a partial phoneme, not a full second phoneme.
