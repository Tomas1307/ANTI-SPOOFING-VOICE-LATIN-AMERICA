How we know the filter is correct
Two scales, same weight: an independent script measured sentence overlap directly and predicted 33.6% unseen; the filter reports 32.3%.
Child matches parent: partial-spoof clips are built from bonafide recordings, so their rate must match bonafide's — and it does (33.2% vs 32.3%).
Why this matters. The same sentence is often read by a training speaker and, separately, by an evaluation speaker (66.4% of eval sentences also appear in training — inherited from shared prompt pools; comparable corpora such as ASVspoof2019 do not even measure this). A detector could gain a small unfair edge on sentences it saw in training. Filtering evaluation to the strict subset removes that doubt entirely: the detector is then tested on speakers it never heard, voices acoustically unlike any it trained on (max cross-split similarity 0.74), and sentences it never saw. Clips that cannot be mapped to their sentence with certainty (4.9%) are left out of the strict subset rather than guessed.
